PROJECT BRIEF / WORKING RESEARCH PROTOTYPE

A wardrobe that starts
with how you feel.

Moodrobe explores whether a short self-reported check-in and a clearer view of an existing wardrobe can make choosing an outfit easier - and help people rediscover clothes they already own.

THE PROBLEM

More clothes do not always mean an easier choice.

The problem hypothesis is a gap between what a person owns, what they can remember and combine, and what feels right for their day. Moodrobe narrows the choice to a few explained combinations from available, owned pieces.

This product interpretation is motivated by the case study and literature. It is not a finding from interviews or a participant study completed for this release.

HOW IT WORKS

A hybrid recommender you can inspect.

PRETRAINED ENCODER

Context as vectors.

A quantised all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence encoder represents the brief and textual garment metadata in 384 dimensions. Inference runs on-device through ONNX Runtime in a Web Worker.

Source model card ↗
LOCAL PREFERENCE HEAD

Choices as examples.

A regularised logistic model learns from accepted and rejected outfit vectors. No examples means a semantic cold start. A few votes do not establish accurate personalisation.

STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINTS

Owned pieces only.

Rules assemble complete outfits and control availability and layering. Ranking combines neural similarity, learned preference and small reuse/occasion terms. Rule-only starter edits are labelled separately.

EVIDENCE, SEPARATED FROM AMBITION

What has actually been demonstrated?

IMPLEMENTEDWorking daily flow

Wardrobe photographs and descriptions, self-report, local neural ranking, preference feedback, saved edits, planning, wear diary and rediscovery.

ENGINEERING CHECKSReproducible verification

Automated tests cover real model loading and feedback training, owned-outfit constraints, local persistence, data migration, media cleanup, reviewer-session isolation and consented usability exports. See the downloadable verification report for the exact release results.

ENGINEERING OBSERVATIONBrief sensitivity

A production smoke test produced different leading edits for contrasting formal and casual briefs with the same sample wardrobe. This confirms execution and input sensitivity, not clothing quality or mood benefit.

NOT YET COLLECTEDParticipant evidence

No recruited usability sessions or controlled product-effectiveness study has been completed for this release. The protocol, recording workflow and local results analyser are prepared; results must come from real participants.

NOT IMPLEMENTEDBiometric inference

Face, voice and heartbeat are not used to infer emotion. The optional local signal tools do not enter the recommendation model. Clothing photographs are displayed, not analysed.

PRODUCT DECISIONS

Small choices with consequences.

Self-report before biometric inference.

The person remains the source of their mood. An unvalidated classifier is not presented as a scientific breakthrough.

Local processing before cloud accounts.

Photos, descriptions and preference examples stay in the browser. Backups are portable; shared browser profiles are not separate accounts.

Reuse before shopping.

The main flow recommends owned clothes and surfaces underused pieces. Purchase-related editorial advice appears only on explicit request.

Testing before stronger claims.

Software checks and usability observations answer different questions. Neither establishes a causal improvement in wellbeing.

THE NEXT REAL-WORLD STEP

A small usability round, then a decision.

Recruit 5–8 consenting adults for an initial usability round, using real wardrobes where feasible. Observe task completion, confusion and whether a person would actually wear a suggestion. Treat observations as design feedback, not a statistically conclusive validation.

The separate, longer mood-versus-control pilot proposed in the case study remains future work. Its proposed thresholds are hypotheses to test, not results already achieved.

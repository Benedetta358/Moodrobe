A PRODUCT CONCEPT BY BENEDETTA

A wardrobe that starts
with how you feel.

Moodrobe explores a simple question: can a mood-aware daily ritual help people choose an outfit and rediscover the clothes they already own?

Working web application · research hypothesis not yet validated

More possibilities.
Fewer decisions.

An outfit recommendation is only useful if it fits the person’s wardrobe and their day. Moodrobe makes owned garments visible, narrows the choice to a few explained combinations and lets the person remain in control.

The working application uses self-reported mood, occasion, temperature and local preference feedback. It does not claim to read a person’s internal emotional state from their appearance or physiology.

01 / AGENCY

Ask before inferring.

A check-in is optional and editable. A person’s account of how they feel takes priority over an unvalidated physiological interpretation.

02 / CIRCULARITY

Use what is already there.

The main recommendation flow never adds shopping products. Underused garments are surfaced for rediscovery, resale or donation.

03 / EVIDENCE

Show the limits.

Real interactions are recorded. No invented participants, model accuracy, clinical outcomes or carbon savings are presented as product results.

HOW THE WORKING PRODUCT MAKES A CHOICE

Transparent by design.

Check in

Choose a mood and adjust energy, emotional tone and tension. Add an occasion and temperature.

Rank

Score available garments using style, colour heuristics, season, time since wear and preference feedback.

Choose

Compare complete outfits with colour palettes. Save, plan or mark a combination as worn.

Learn

Positive and negative feedback update local garment weights. The diary records actual user actions.

DELIVERED CAPABILITIES AND RESEARCH BOUNDARIES

What exists today.

WORKING

Personal digital wardrobeLocal photo upload and compression, a central-image colour estimate for user confirmation, manual categories, search, filters and garment availability. Sixteen clearly marked illustrated garments support an immediate demonstration.

WORKING

Daily outfit experienceSelf-report check-in, manual or opt-in local weather, deterministic ranking, colour palettes, feedback, saved looks, outfit planning and a wear diary.

WORKING

Reuse and data controlIn-app reminders for garments unused for 60 days, external reuse links, backup export/import, deletion and separate consent preferences. Data stays in the current browser; there is no cloud account or cross-device sync.

EXPERIMENTAL TOOLS

Local signal explorationA camera mirror, ephemeral five-second audio recording and RMSSD calculation from imported RR intervals. These tools do not infer emotion and do not feed the recommendation engine.

FUTURE RESEARCH

Multimodal emotion inferenceFacial-expression modelling, wearable integration, vocal-prosody analysis and signal fusion require technical implementation, scientific validation and a data-protection assessment. None is represented as a finished capability.

FUTURE SERVICES

Automated vision and commercial infrastructureBackground segmentation, AI garment classification, brand QR scanning, subscriptions, cloud authentication and retail partnerships are outside the delivered implementation.

A TESTABLE NEXT STEP

Build the evidence,
not just the interface.

The proposed pilot recruits 15–20 participants for two to three weeks to compare mood-motivated suggestions with a control. The case study specifies a 60% preference threshold, 70% check-in adherence criterion and rediscovery of an unused garment by at least one third of participants.

The app includes a separately consented, local A/B collection mechanism and CSV export to illustrate the workflow. Its control uses different ranking parameters and randomised display order. This is an exploratory interface exercise, not a controlled trial or evidence of effectiveness. No pilot outcomes have been claimed.

Colour psychology and enclothed cognition motivate questions for investigation; they do not establish that a particular palette will change an individual’s mood.
